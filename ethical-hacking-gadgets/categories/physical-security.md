# Physical Security Tools (Locks & Bypass)

Physical penetration testing hardware: lockpicks, bypass tools, bump keys, and
disc-detainer picks. Locksport is a legitimate hobby and physical-pentest is a
real profession — but the legal line here is sharp.

> ⚠️ In many jurisdictions, possessing lockpicks *with intent* to commit a crime
> is itself an offence, and picking a lock you don't own/aren't authorised to
> open is breaking-and-entering. Know your local law before buying or carrying.
> See [../ETHICS_AND_LEGAL.md](../ETHICS_AND_LEGAL.md).

## Capabilities & possibilities
- **Picking / raking:** manipulate pin-tumbler and wafer locks open non-destructively.
- **Bypass:** open doors/padlocks/key-boxes *without* engaging the lock cylinder
  (latch slipping, shims, under-door tools) — often faster than picking.
- **Bumping:** use specially-cut keys + a tap to jog pins open.
- **Disc-detainer picking:** specialist tools for tubular/disc locks.
- **Key duplication:** mould/cast a copy of a key you're authorised to duplicate.
- **Training:** cutaway cylinders and practice locks to build skill legally.

## Legitimate uses
- Authorised **physical penetration tests** (with a signed scope/RoE and,
  ideally, a "get-out-of-jail" authorisation letter to carry on-site).
- **Locksport** — a legal hobby of picking locks you own for sport/skill.
- Locksmithing and lockout recovery on **your own** property.
- Security assessment: proving a facility's locks/doors are inadequate so they
  can be upgraded.
- Awareness: showing why cheap padlocks and unshielded latches aren't security.

## Limits & the law
- **Possession laws vary widely** — some places treat picks as burglary tools if
  intent is inferred; others are permissive for hobbyists. Check before carrying.
- Picking/bypassing any lock you don't own or aren't authorised to open is a
  crime, full stop.
- On a real engagement, carry your **authorisation letter** and stay strictly in
  scope (named doors, hours, buildings).
- These are **skill** tools — competence comes from practice on your own locks,
  not from owning expensive picks.

## Starter path (locksport, legally)
1. Buy a **practice/cutaway lock** and a basic pick set — pick locks you own.
2. Learn single-pin picking on progressively-pinned practice locks.
3. Understand bypass concepts (they're often the real-world shortcut).
4. If going pro: get formal physical-pentest training and always work under written authorisation.
