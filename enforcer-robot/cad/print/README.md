# Print plan — everything the robot needs, in the order to print it

Two folders, two provenances:

- **`sesame/`** — 11 parts, vendored unmodified from
  [dorianborian/sesame-robot](https://github.com/dorianborian/sesame-robot)
  at commit `86d2bf8`, Apache 2.0. See [`NOTICE`](NOTICE).
- **`enforcer/`** — 6 parts, generated by [`../make_stl.py`](../make_stl.py).

**17 distinct parts, 23 prints** (some are quantity >1), about **125 g** of
PLA. One 1 kg spool covers the whole robot four times over.

---

## Before anything: the 20 mm cube

New printer, first print. Not the robot.

Measure the cube with calipers. **Not within ~0.3 mm → stop and calibrate**,
because Sesame's joints hold servo splines and a machine that is 0.4 mm out
will produce eight joints that all need reprinting. Twenty minutes now
against four hours later.

This is the Days 1–2 gate in [`../../BUILD_CHECKLIST.md`](../../BUILD_CHECKLIST.md)
and it has not moved just because the printer is new and shiny.

## Settings

From Sesame's own printing guide — these are theirs, not guesses:

| | |
|---|---|
| Material | PLA / PLA+ |
| Infill | **8–10%** |
| Wall loops | **2** |
| Infill pattern | Honeycomb |
| Layer height | 0.2 mm |

**Supports: only the top cover.** Everything else prints unsupported. The top
cover wants *outer brim only* and **Normal (Manual)** supports — upstream has
photos of exactly where, at
`hardware/printing/README.md` in their repo. Look at them before slicing.

**Joints: use auto-orient.** Upstream says the tool places all eight correctly.

## The order, plate by plate

Five plates. Numbers 1–3 can all run today; 4 and 5 wait on a tape measure
and a scale.

| # | Plate | Supports | est. |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 20 mm calibration cube | no | 20 min |
| 1 | **one** joint — `R1-v117` | no | 11 min |
| 2 | remaining 7 joints + 4× `cliff_bracket` + `nozzle_mount` | no | ≈ 1.9 h |
| 3 | `Internal-Frame-v121` + `Bottom-Cover-v121` | no | ≈ 1.6 h |
| 4 | `Top-Cover-Enclosed-v117` | **yes** | ≈ 50 min |
| — | *assemble, get it walking, weigh it, measure it* | | |
| 5 | `payload_deck` + `camera_mount` + 2× `reservoir_cradle` | no | ≈ 1.1 h |

**Plate 1 is one joint, on purpose.** Test-fit an MG90S in it before
committing to the other seven. A servo pocket that is 0.2 mm tight is a
fifteen-minute discovery here and a four-hour one after the full plate. If
the servos have not arrived yet, print it anyway and check it against the
calipers and the upstream render — you still catch gross scaling errors.

**Plate 4 is alone because its settings differ.** Outer brim and Normal
(Manual) supports are plate-wide in most slicers, and you do not want either
on the joints.

**Nothing after plate 4 should start before the robot walks.** Stock firmware
first — that is how you know a later fault is yours and not an assembly
error, and it is why the payload parts wait.

## Batch 1 — the body (plates 1–4)

| Part | Qty | g | est. |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R1-v117` `R2-v117` `L1-v117` `L2-v117` | 4 | 17 | 44 min |
| `R3-v117` `R4-v117` `L3-v117` `L4-v117` | 4 | 16 | 44 min |
| `Internal-Frame-v121` | 1 | 24 | 63 min |
| `Bottom-Cover-v121` | 1 | 12 | 32 min |
| `Top-Cover-Enclosed-v117` | 1 | 18 | 49 min |
| | **11** | **87 g** | **≈ 3.9 h** |

**Put all 8 joints on one plate.** Not just for convenience: printed
individually, parts this small hit the slicer's minimum-layer-time and the
printer spends most of each layer waiting for plastic to cool. Batched, that
dead time disappears into the travel between parts. One plate is both faster
and better-cooled than eight separate prints.

Three top-cover styles exist upstream. **Enclosed v117** is the one to take —
it is the current design, has the magnetic hat mount, and covers the display
wiring. `Cat` and `No-Ears` are older.

After this batch you can assemble Sesame, flash stock firmware, and get it
walking. That is Weeks 1–2 of the checklist and it needs none of the parts
below.

## Batch 2 — the Enforcer payload (plate 5, print AFTER measuring)

⚠️ **Do not print these tonight.** Three of the six have dimensions that are
currently estimates, marked as such in `make_stl.py`, and the measurements
come off the assembled Sesame and the actual bottle:

| Constant | Now | Where the real number comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `DECK_L` × `DECK_W` | 90 × 60 mm | measure the printed top cover |
| `BOTTLE_D` | 36 mm | measure a UMETASS 60 ml bottle |
| `SESAME_MASS_G` | 380 g | weigh the assembled robot |

Set them, re-run `python3 make_stl.py --test`, *then* print:

| Part | Qty | g | est. | Waiting on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `payload_deck` | 1 | 14 | 37 min | `DECK_L`/`DECK_W` |
| `reservoir_cradle` | 2 | 6 | 16 min | `BOTTLE_D` |
| `cliff_bracket` | 4 | 6 | 16 min | nothing — print with batch 1 if you like |
| `nozzle_mount` | 1 | 3 | 7 min | nothing |
| `camera_mount` | 1 | 4 | 10 min | see below |
| | **9** | **33 g** | **≈ 1.4 h** | |

`payload_deck` is the one part everything else bolts to, and it is the one
whose dimensions are guessed. Printing it first is how you print it twice.

`cliff_bracket` and `nozzle_mount` depend on nothing — add them to batch 1's
plate if there is room.

### The camera mount has an open question against it

`camera_mount` fixes the camera at the same +20° as the nozzle, and that
pairing is deliberate: the robot yaws to centre the target and the vertical
angle is mechanical, so if the two disagree it aims high or low by exactly
the difference.

But the perception work measured that **a phone held in the hand leaves the
frame** at the real lens angle, while a phone lying on the desk does not —
the opposite of what the robot needs. See
[`../../perception/README.md`](../../perception/README.md). The fix is
framing, and the framing is this bracket.

Print it, mount it, and **re-record a few minutes of footage through the real
camera before committing to it**. It is a 10-minute, 4 g part; finding out
after the Week 4 shell restyle is the expensive version.

## Not printing: `phone_tray`

The torque budget settled this, and `make_stl.py --test` prints the numbers:
a phone is ~180 g against Sesame's ~380 g, which busts the leg budget at any
walkable stance. **The robot does not carry the phone** — it guards it, and
Warden mode is guard-and-block. See
[`../../BEHAVIOURS.md`](../../BEHAVIOURS.md).

The tray survives only as an optional prop for one video shot, carried empty.
36 min and 14 g if you want it. It is not part of the robot.

## About these time estimates

Computed from each STL's volume and surface area against Sesame's own print
settings, at an assumed 5 mm³/s effective flow. **Treat them as ±40%** and as
useful for ordering the work, not for planning an afternoon.

Your slicer knows your machine, your acceleration limits and your cooling.
Its number is the real one — this table just tells you which batch is the
long pole before you have sliced anything.

The generator is [`../make_stl.py`](../make_stl.py) for the enforcer parts.
Regenerate rather than editing STLs; they are build output, and the geometry
self-test (`--test`) checks every emitted solid is watertight before writing.
